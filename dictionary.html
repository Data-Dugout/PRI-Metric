Explanation of Terms

Every stat, abbreviation and metric used across the site is explained here.

PRI (Progression or Regression Index)
A 0–100 score that estimates how much of a hitter's season was driven by skill versus luck, and how likely they are to trend up or down the following year. 100 is top-tier progression (very unlucky, due to improve), 50 is roughly neutral, 0 is due for serious regression (very lucky, due to decline).
xBA (Expected Batting Average)
Expected Batting Average (xBA) is a Statcast metric that measures the likelihood that a batted ball will become a hit. Each batted ball is assigned an xBA based on how often comparable balls — in terms of exit velocity, launch angle and, on certain types of batted balls, Sprint Speed — have become hits since Statcast was implemented Major League wide in 2015.
xSLG (Expected Slugging Percentage)
In the same way that each batted ball is assigned an expected batting average, every batted ball is given a single, double, triple and home run probability based on the results of comparable batted balls since Statcast was implemented Major League wide in 2015. For the majority of batted balls, this is achieved using only exit velocity and launch angle.
xwOBA (Expected Weighted On-Base Average)
The most all-encompassing of the three expected stats. Expected Weighted On-Base Average (xwOBA) measures a hitter's total offensive value per plate appearance based entirely on the quality of contact (exit velocity and launch angle), walks, hit-by-pitches, and strikeouts — while completely removing defense, ballpark dimensions, and luck from the equation.
wOBA (Weighted On-Base Average)
A rate stat that weighs every way of reaching base (walk, single, double, home run, etc.) by how much it actually contributes to scoring runs, rather than treating all hits equally the way batting average does.
BABIP (Batting Average on Balls in Play)
Batting average counting only balls put in play (excludes strikeouts, walks, and home runs). League-average BABIP sits close to .300 almost every season, which makes it a useful quick gut-check for luck — a hitter running far above or below .300 on balls in play is usually due for some correction. Unlike the other three components in PRI, BABIP has no "expected" counterpart, so it's scaled directly against that season's range rather than compared to a projection.
MAX / RANGE
For each metric, MAX is the single biggest "luck" gap posted by any qualified hitter that season (i.e., however lucky the luckiest hitter in baseball was). RANGE is the distance between the unluckiest and luckiest hitter that year (MAX minus MIN) — the full spread of luck that happened across the league. Both are recalculated every season, which is why PRI scores aren't meant to be compared across different years.
wRC+ (Weighted Runs Created Plus)
A park- and league-adjusted offensive stat where 100 is exactly league-average; each point above or below 100 represents 1% better or worse than average. PRI uses wRC+ as a filter on the results (separating out already-elite hitters, who tend to improve less even when unlucky), not as an input to the score itself.
PA (Plate Appearances)
Every trip a hitter takes to the plate, including walks and sacrifices (a broader count than "at-bats"). PRI includes all players with 100+ PA in a given season as its qualification threshold.
Hard-Hit Rate
Statcast defines a "hard-hit ball" as one hit with an exit velocity of 95 mph or higher, and a player's hard-hit rate is simply the percentage of batted balls that were hit at 95 mph or more.
Barrel Rate
The percentage of batted balls that result in a barrel. To be barreled, a batted ball requires an exit velocity of at least 98 mph. At that speed, balls struck with a launch angle between 26–30 degrees always garner Barreled classification. For every mph over 98, the range of launch angles expands.
Exit Velocity
Exit Velocity measures the speed of the baseball as it comes off the bat, immediately after a batter makes contact. This is tracked for all Batted Ball Events — outs, hits and errors.
