Phase 2: Threshold Testing

After testing the overall accuracy, I identified and studied the outliers. I discovered that a significant number of players with a score above 60 who did not improve had a high wRC+ the year prior.

Take Juan Soto in 2024. His PRI score of 65.5 signaled that he was due for progression, but he also posted a wRC+ of about 180 that year. That doesn't mean he couldn't have been better, but a jump from that level is rare. Limiting progression candidates to hitters with a wRC+ of 100 or lower improved accuracy immensely, and tightening the cutoff to 90 or lower improved it even further.

The next tests we conducted were to find what thresholds can strengthen the prediction. This is independent of the PRI score itself, but rather strengthens the confidence. For example, a player with a 70 PRI has about a 75% chance to increase their wRC+ in the next season. But if you add in a wRC+ threshold or a plate appearance threshold, the odds get increasingly higher. These are very useful to use in addition to PRI.

Why 60 and 40?

Phase 1 showed where the signal is strongest. Players with a PRI above 60 improved their wRC+ at least 59% of the time, and that jumped to 78% in the 70–80 range. At the other end, players at 40 and below improved only 25% of the time or less. The middle is much weaker: players in the 50–60 range improved just 53% of the time, close to a coin flip. So these tests count players above 60 as progression candidates and players at 40 and below as regression candidates.

Importance of wRC+ thresholds

I tested two threshold levels, using the prior-year wRC+ to filter PRI predictions. The first was the league average of 100. The second tightened it to 90 and 110. These tests filtered players with a PRI above 60 as progression candidates, and players with a PRI of 40 and below as regression candidates.

Putting it into practice

So, a practical use would go something like this: Jac Caglianone had a wRC+ of just 46 in 2025 and a PRI score of 100. The addition of a low wRC+ greatly strengthens my prediction that he will improve in offensive production in 2026 (which he did).

Importance of PA thresholds

Accuracy improves the more plate appearances a hitter gets the following season. More playing time means a bigger sample size, and a bigger sample pulls a hitter's results closer to their expected metrics. It's simple math in action: the more at-bats a player gets, the less luck can distort the outcome, and the more room the prediction has to come true.
