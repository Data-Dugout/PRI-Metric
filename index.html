PRI (Progression or Regression Index) predicts and measures a player's offensive changes from year to year. The metric is built on a combination of expected stats that have been back tested for five years.

▼ Expected Stats Explanation

Expected stats are a Statcast metric used by MLB to strip defense, ballpark, and luck out of the equation. If a hitter squares up a ball and an outfielder makes a leaping catch at the wall, the box score shows an out — a .000 result on that swing. But based on that ball's exit velocity, launch angle, and barrel classification, batted balls with that exact profile land for a hit about 70% of the time. That 70% is the player's expected outcome on that swing, independent of what actually happened to it.

Over a full season — and especially across many seasons — actual results tend to drift back toward those expected numbers, the same way any sample converges toward its underlying probability the longer you run it.
