About

Why this exists

PRI was developed entirely from the work of Cody Hamilton. Cody is a self-published baseball writer and researcher. You can find most of his work on Substack at thedatadugout.substack.com.

PRI started with the use of expected stats. We often hear players, analysts or anyone in the baseball media world talking about a player's expected batting average or expected home runs. But what does that really mean and how do you use it? Sure, it's easy to understand that if a player has an expected batting average of .300 but a real average of .250, they're getting the short end of the stick.

But PRI takes multiple expected metrics and scales the score from 0–100 to not only tell if a player is lucky/unlucky but how severe it is.

PRI was built, tested, re-built & re-tested over and over. Now, with over five seasons of data, we stand at a great place to implement the metric and use it in assessing a player's likelihood to progress or regress (PRI).
